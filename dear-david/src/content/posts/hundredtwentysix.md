Because it just felt too hard, like the person was there, and you loved them, but you didn't know that you were pushing them away, by sending too many messages. Then, you realise, you are going through a crisis, and maybe the person, is a help-line, and then you relax, knowing that maybe the person isn't your average Joe-soap, willing, to judge you and put a flyer on it. There is no cure, to that needy, addiction, feeling, when you have crashed, and realise your last life-line isn't a hook-up, a boy-or-girl friend, or even a mate, but something more. Maybe just someone that is there at the other side that has been through their tides and woes. Then, there is a little crumb, thinking, maybe this man loves me? Maybe all my grieving and texting and dumping is relatable, and maybe what I am feeling isn't so much me, but both of us, and that there is a component that he feels abandoned and worthless too, and maybe just as addicted to the text garble or text messaging as other people? Then, you realise, you really do need help, but put it up for all to see. That being addicted to text messaging someone, or a help line, that is not a help line, even if it's someone attractive or someone you think you have a chance with, is just a deep connection that goes beyond 'definitions' that the text garble, the unrelatable, relatability, is just emotional attachment, and THAT is painful -- but is it worth exploring? Yes, and NO. No, that without boundaries, the next and next text message or message becomes pure and pure panic, that you need help, and help needs to become available. There comes a time, when you have sunk so low, hospitalisation or arrest, becomes, the only approachable relatable thing that makes sense. And it's barely out of reach, or has already happened, then it's no longer an off-topic measure, or something that is inapproachable, but something that has become relatable, because life is suffering. The thing is, do you have a rock solid person, that deals with your position or situation admirably, or do you need a wall too?
